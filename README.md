Wearable sensors are of great significance in healthcare and rehabilitation, especially in the recent years. These miniature computers are capable of capturing body motions and gathering relevant data, which helps to know more of the daily routine of a person i.e. walking, running, sitting, standing, or lying down. Doctors and other medical workers can use this information to keep track of patients, particularly older adults or those who have sustained injuries. It can also be applied in intelligent systems who can learn through human behaviour and react to it. As the deep learning is fast growing, the analysis of complex data gathered by such sensors has become easier. With deep learning models, time-series data can automatically learn patterns to enhance the accuracy of activity recognition without excessive feature design. This dissertation utilises deep learning to identify physical activities based on the PAMAP2 Physical Activity Monitoring dataset. The size of this dataset is large and utilised a lot by the researchers and the data obtained is collected by using numerous sensors attached to various sections of the human body like the wrist, chest, and ankle. There is a first step of cleaning the sensor data which is of high frequency and then releasing missing values, normalisation of the values, division into smaller fragments appropriate to train is done. I will then utilise and compare the various deep learning models of time series classification, including convolutional neural networks as well as recurrent neural networks. To assess the performance of these models, I use common metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall and F1-score to comprehend the performance of these models. An interactive system is also created by me to show how the proposed method operates in a real-life scenario. The outcomes of this project may be instrumental in the creation of viable and scalable activity recognition systems, which may be implemented in the hospitals, home care, and fitness monitoring and sports analysis (among other real life) healthcare systems in future.
